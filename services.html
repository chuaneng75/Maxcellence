Services

Three practice areas. One method underneath.

Every engagement, regardless of subject, moves through the same four stages in the same order. The consistency is deliberate — it's how the founder keeps every engagement to the same standard, whichever client she's working with.

The method, in full

01 / Diagnose

Two weeks inside the operation

We sit with operators, not just the executives who hired us — on the floor, on the sales calls, in the finance close. The goal is a single sentence naming the actual constraint, distinct from the symptom the board brought us in to treat.

02 / Design

One recommendation, pressure-tested

We build the path we'd take if it were our own company, then spend as long trying to break it as we spent building it. The client receives one position, not a menu — along with the written record of every alternative we ruled out and why.

03 / Deliver

Presented to the people who'll live with it

Mayple presents the findings in person to the owners or investors, walks through the numbers under direct questioning, and puts the recommendation in writing. We do not deliver findings by report alone.

04 / Sustain

One quarter of standing follow-through

Included in every engagement: continued access to Mayple after implementation begins, through the first real test of the plan — a tough quarter, a supplier issue, an early sign something needs adjusting.

Practice areas

Where we apply it.

Corporate strategy

Ownership & direction

Portfolio and divestiture decisions, market entry and exit, capital allocation disputes between founders and boards. The work that ends with a single decisive move, not an ongoing initiative.

Risk & resilience

Exposure & response

Supply chain concentration, regulatory exposure, and the operating decisions boards make after a near-miss forces the question of what else could fail the same way.

Governance

Succession & structure

Founder transitions, board composition, and the succession plans that are easy to postpone until the year they can't be. We're often called in by the outgoing leader, not the incoming one.
