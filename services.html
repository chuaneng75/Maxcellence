Three pillars. One method underneath.

Every engagement, regardless of pillar, moves through the same four stages in the same order. The consistency is deliberate — it's how the founder keeps every engagement to the same standard, whichever client she's working with.

The MAXcellence Method, in full

01 / Diagnose

Understand the business, the numbers, the bottlenecks

We sit with the business, not just the owner — the books, the operations, the reporting as it actually exists today. The goal is a clear diagnosis: where visibility is missing, where cost is leaking, and what's really constraining growth.

02 / Structure

Build the reporting, SOPs, controls and automation

The business model, chart of accounts, reporting pack, SOPs and internal controls the business will actually run on — practical, and sized to the business, not a template borrowed from a much larger company.

03 / Monitor

Track cash flow, margins, KPIs and execution

Mayple walks the owner through the numbers directly, in plain terms, on a regular cadence — not a report left in an inbox. Decisions get made in that conversation, not after it.

04 / Scale

Support expansion, restructuring, funding or overseas growth

Once the foundation holds, MAXcellence supports the next stage directly — fundraising support, investor reporting, feasibility studies, or the operational groundwork for expanding into a new market such as Indonesia.

The three pillars

Where the method gets applied.

Pillar 01 — Strategy & Business Modelling

Business Diagnostic · Business Modelling · Restructuring

Problem — the business is growing, or about to, but no one can say with confidence whether the model actually works, where it's profitable, or what it needs before it can raise capital or expand.

Outcome — a clear business model, profitability visibility, funding readiness and a practical growth roadmap.

Ideal for — founders, investors, new ventures, expanding businesses.

Pillar 02 — Finance Transformation & Reporting

Consolidated Financial Overview · Management Reporting (FP&A) · Fractional CFO

Problem — the books close late, numbers across entities don't tie out cleanly, and there's no monthly rhythm for actually reading what the business is telling you.

What you receive — monthly P&L, balance sheet and cash-flow review; budget vs actual analysis; a rolling 13-week cash-flow forecast; a KPI dashboard; a monthly management review; an action list for margin, cost and cash improvements.

Typical timeline — 2–4 weeks to stand up, then ongoing on a monthly cadence.

Pillar 03 — Process, Automation & Control

Simple Automation Tools · SOP Set-Up · Process Improvement

Problem — month-end close takes too long, the same manual errors keep recurring, and it's unclear who owns what once the business grows past the founder doing everything.

Outcome — faster month-end close, fewer manual errors, clearer accountability and scalable operations.

Ideal for — SMEs, villa owners, F&B operators, property developers scaling past founder-only operations.
